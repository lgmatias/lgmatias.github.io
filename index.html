John Matiasevich

Cybersecurity engineering & IT support · M.Eng Cybersecurity Engineering, University of Maryland · B.S. Computer Science, Georgetown University

Located in Georgetown, Texas, USA

Hello! I'm John, a cybersecurity engineering graduate student with a background in computer science and hands-on IT support training through coursework and certifications. I enjoy working at the intersection of security, systems, and practical tooling, whether that means practicing access troubleshooting in labs, building small automations, or experimenting with low-level vulnerabilities in a lab environment.

I’m currently pursuing a Master of Engineering in Cybersecurity Engineering at the University of Maryland, where I work on writing and attacking secure C programs, embedded security on STM32 boards, and hands-on projects in advanced networking, firewalls, and intrusion detection systems. Before that, I earned my B.S. in Computer Science from Georgetown University.

I took on a fixed fee engagement with the Texas Legal Services Center as an IT Security Contractor. It covers an access control audit and remediation of LegalServer, which is the case management platform holding client and case data for a statewide legal aid nonprofit. The work has three parts.

  • Auditing permissions across a few dozen user roles against what each job actually requires, then delivering written least privilege recommendations and flagging high privilege capabilities for revocation or consolidation.
  • Reviewing user to role assignments for every active account against the personnel roster, then delivering exception lists for the overprivileged and out of date ones.
  • Scoping report visibility by role across a library of several hundred reports, and neutralizing inactive reports that amount to a parallel access path to client data.

With the Democratic National Committee (DNC), I maintain and operate the security alerting pipeline I built during my internship there. It integrates the Expel Workbench API with Google Apps Script and the Slack Web API, and its custom routing logic more than halved mean time to resolve. During the internship I triaged Expel SOC alerts alongside the security team using CrowdStrike XDR. I managed the pipeline’s secrets and scoped its API credentials. I also authored the SOPs covering its configuration and operation. I’ve since stayed on as a Cybersecurity Super Volunteer, keeping the service reliable and researching cloud security posture and entitlement controls in Wiz (CSPM / CIEM) to scope a prospective cloud security project.

Previously, I worked at Greysteel as a Data Analyst, where I supported staff by resolving system access and security-related issues, authored internal troubleshooting guides, and automated Salesforce data uploads using Python and APIs, which saved 40+ hours per week while reducing errors. I also partnered with IT to escalate and resolve incidents involving account permissions and data integrity.

Earlier, as a cofounder at Kleinbrook Investment Management real estate wholesaling company, I owned the access control and AWS side of our lead generation pipeline. Scraped property and ownership data landed in S3, and I scoped the IAM policies and recordkeeping permissions around it. I also trained colleagues on secure workflows for handling sensitive client data.

Outside of work and coursework, I learn by building and breaking things on hardware I own. My current focus is a privacy hardening build across a ThinkPad and a GrapheneOS phone, aiming at a strong privacy posture with minimal trust given to proprietary software. The piece I have written up is self managed end to end encrypted cloud storage in AWS, a personal drive where the provider only ever holds ciphertext. Privacy hardening is an important part of security that the enterprise side of the industry does not explore much.

I also keep a home “blue team” lab on Windows 10 that simulates enterprise style environments. I use Process Monitor and Wireshark and Regshot to run vulnerability scans and write detection and response playbooks. I practice binary exploitation and reverse engineering through CTF platforms like pwnable and HackTheBox.

Earlier projects include a Discord bot that queried the Bungie API and presented game data in a clean, human-readable format, as well as reinforcement learning agents built with PyTorch and OpenAI Gym for environments like Cart-Pole and Ant-v2. These projects strengthened my skills in Python, REST APIs, data processing, and reinforcement learning workflows.

Some of the things I’m currently interested in:

  • Secure coding and vulnerability research in C (buffer overflows, format strings, and exploit development fundamentals).
  • Cloud and network security projects that translate into real-world defense (AWS, network security, and monitoring).
  • Automation and data tooling with Python, Bash, and REST APIs to remove repetitive work.
  • Incident detection and response playbooks informed by hands-on lab exercises and threat simulation.
  • Practical IAM and IT support: role-based access control, least-privilege review, account lifecycle, and clear communication with the users affected.

If you’re working on something related to cybersecurity, IT support, or automation and think I might be a good fit to collaborate, feel free to reach out!
